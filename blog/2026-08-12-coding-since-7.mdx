i wrote my first lines of code when i was **7**. today i'm 18, so that's
eleven years of "quick experiments" that sometimes turn into real projects.

{/* truncate */}

## what actually mattered

**1. copy everything, at first.**
my early "projects" were tutorials with the names changed. that's fine. copying
is how your hands learn the syntax. just don't stop there — change one thing,
break it, fix it.

**2. finish something boring.**
i have a graveyard of exciting half-projects. the thing that taught me the
most was a boring tool that i actually deployed and used for a month. finish
line > perfect line.

**3. build for a real person.**
`AyudaEnCali` stopped being a react exercise the moment i thought "my neighbor
needs to find a shelter with a bad signal and 5% battery". every fallback in
that app comes from that image, not from a blog post.

**4. write tests when you're scared, not when you're confident.**
in the fire detection project the test suite existed because i had 48 hours and
no room to find out the websocket was broken. green check = keep going.

**5. linux.**
not because it's cool (ok, partly), but because nothing teaches you how a
computer works like breaking your own setup on a friday night.

## what i'm doing now

- maintaining **AyudaEnCali** and **Áureo**
- learning **pytorch** and **react native**
- shipping open source stuff around ethereum and ai
- trying to reply to messages faster than once a month

if you're 7, 12 or 17 and reading this: you don't need permission, you need a
terminal and one idea that annoys you enough to fix it.
